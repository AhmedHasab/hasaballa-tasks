02 Second priority · Separate from performance transfer

Behavioral Identity Engine — BIE

Preserve how each person habitually behaves—not just how they look or sound. Follow the existing BIE proposal and the development team’s written assessment.

BIE = a character’s persistent behavioral identity. Face and voice alone are not enough. The third layer preserves the distinctive ways a character looks, reacts, moves, laughs, pauses and communicates from scene to scene. The separate Performance Transfer Studio (priority 09) copies a particular source performance; it does not create the enduring behavioral profile.

Behavior that matters

Eye focus and gaze, eyebrow habits, facial expressions, head movement, hand gestures, body posture, walking style, smile and laughter, speech rhythm, pauses, reaction timing, emotional reactions and repeated personal mannerisms.

Input: optional behavior references

The Character Pack may include reference images, reference voice and optional reference behavior videos: interviews, ordinary speech, public appearances, conversations and walking footage. These are used to build a structured Motion Profile for the same character.

Motion Profile pipeline

Analyze reference footage, extract eyes, facial landmarks, gesture frequencies, head movements and timing information; save it as a reusable profile; and pass appropriate behavioral signals to video animation/generation.

Where it belongs

Integrate with Smart Director Automatic, Smart Director Manual, Image Animation and Video Generation. The behavior references remain optional. Without them, existing animation remains available.

Character Pack levels

Level 1: reference images → visual identity.
Level 2: images + voice → visual and voice identity.
Level 3: images + voice + behavioral footage → visual, voice and behavioral identity.

Development team’s assessment

The Indian team described roughly 8–10 additional weeks for analysis, Motion Profiles, generation conditioning and tool integration. It proposed humans first. Non-human behavioral consistency comparable to proprietary cloud examples would require further work.

Offline equipment and model options

The team assessed a 24 GB RTX 5090 workstation as sufficient for its proposed offline approach. It mentioned MediaPipe, OpenFace, Hallo2, MuseTalk, AnimateDiff, Wan2.1 and SkyReels V2 as candidate components. Mentioning a model is not proof of equivalent output quality or confirmed delivery.

Research and validation boundary

The engineering reply distinguished implementation from training: pretrained components may be integrated without training a new model; deeper character-specific accuracy could require separate fine-tuning. Any newer or alternative components must be benchmarked for quality before adoption.

Original proposed non-human scope versus initial engineering scope

The proposal describes humans, cartoons, clay figures, animals, fictional personalities, humanized places, objects, countries and concepts. The vendor’s proposed first stage covers human characters; do not claim those non-human BIE capabilities already exist at the required fidelity. This scope boundary does not remove the separately specified Living Objects Studio.

Source basis: “Hasaballa AI – Behavioral Identity Engine (20-page proposal)”, “Response to Follow-Up Questions – Behavioral Identity Engine (3-page development-team reply)” and the supplied BIE illustration. The owner’s objective

Create emerging specialist models—Hasaballa Text, ImageGen, Animator and Voice—and train and evaluate them over approximately one or two years of continuous platform use. The long-term objective is to adopt them as primary alternatives when they genuinely outperform models such as SDXL, FLUX and existing animation/lip-sync and voice models.

04 Original roadmap · Task 20

Auto Smart Fix

Detect post-generation mistakes in text, faces, lighting and visual integrity, then let the user fix or dismiss each issue.

Inspect generated scenes before they reach the viewer. The tool identifies mistakes and offers a controlled fix or an option to ignore a finding without regenerating the entire approved video.

Official Task 20 reference image from the original HASABALLA AI roadmap

What Auto Smart Fix examines

Arabic and English text errors in signs, newspapers and on-screen writing; incorrect or inconsistent faces against references; lighting, color and saturation mistakes; and visibly distorted or broken elements.

What the user controls

Present a clear list of detected problems. Offer a one-click correction when possible, or allow the user to ignore an acceptable result. The original Task 20 places this tool before Selective Smart Edit (Task 11) and associates it with Hasaballa Engine (Task 19).

09 Integrated studio · Distinct from BIE

Advanced Performance Transfer Studio

Make a chosen character execute the movements and expressions of another specific performance while keeping the character’s appearance.

Transfer a particular performance to a different target character while preserving the target’s visual identity. The specified examples are Mandela behaving as Nasser, a woman executing Shakira’s choreography, or Tom Cruise performing a Michael Jackson-style movement sequence. This is a performance requirement—not the creation of a recurring BIE profile.

Performance Transfer versus BIE

BIE · stable identity

Learns and preserves habitual movements, expressions, gaze, speaking patterns and recurring behavioral mannerisms across scenes.

Advanced Performance Transfer · one specific performance

Takes a selected source performance and transfers its timing, movements, expressions and physical acting to another selected character in a particular scene.

The studio may use common animation, audio and lip-sync services without developing a second permanent-behavior-profile engine. The supplied Version 2 instructions list 6.jpg and 7.jpg for this studio; they do not specify an original video for it, so none has been invented or moved from another category.
