Behavior that matters

Eye focus and gaze, eyebrow habits, facial expressions, head movement, hand gestures, body posture, walking style, smile and laughter, speech rhythm, pauses, reaction timing, emotional reactions and repeated personal mannerisms.

Input: optional behavior references

The Character Pack may include reference images, reference voice and optional reference behavior videos: interviews, ordinary speech, public appearances, conversations and walking footage. These are used to build a structured Motion Profile for the same character.

Motion Profile pipeline

Analyze reference footage, extract eyes, facial landmarks, gesture frequencies, head movements and timing information; save it as a reusable profile; and pass appropriate behavioral signals to video animation/generation.

Where it belongs

Integrate with Smart Director Automatic, Smart Director Manual, Image Animation and Video Generation. The behavior references remain optional. Without them, existing animation remains available.

Character Pack levels

Level 1: reference images → visual identity.
Level 2: images + voice → visual and voice identity.
Level 3: images + voice + behavioral footage → visual, voice and behavioral identity.

Development team’s assessment

The development team is working on improving and implementing BIE enhancements, including Motion Profiles, generation quality, behavioral consistency, and integration with the platform’s animation and production tools. Its earlier engineering estimate was roughly 8–10 additional weeks for analysis, conditioning and integration; this estimate is not a confirmed delivery date.

Offline equipment and model options

The owner’s objective

Create emerging specialist models - Hasaballa GPT, Hasaballa Image Generator, Hasaballa Image Animator, and Hasaballa TTS - and train and evaluate them over approximately one or two years of continuous platform use. Each Hasaballa model must be trained using relevant, quality-evaluated outputs from the corresponding models and tools already operating within the platform: image-generation model outputs train Hasaballa Image Generator; text-to-speech model outputs train Hasaballa TTS; image-animation and motion-generation outputs train Hasaballa Image Animator; and text-generation outputs train Hasaballa GPT. Apply this same category-specific learning approach to any additional specialist models. The long-term objective is to make HASABALLA models eligible for intelligent generation rotation once they demonstrate strong competitive performance against models such as SDXL, FLUX and existing animation, lip-sync and voice models, while keeping all existing models available.

Intelligent Model Rotation and Future Commercial Vision

The platform will retain all its existing models and tools. After extensive training and rigorous quality evaluation demonstrate that a HASABALLA specialist model can compete effectively with the corresponding models already available, the platform will be able to automatically select and rotate between HASABALLA and existing models for generation based on each task and proven output quality. Original models will remain available and will not be removed or permanently replaced.

The long-term ambition is also to offer mature HASABALLA models as paid, credit-based generation services. Each specialist model could be offered through its own dedicated website, or a future standalone Hasaballa Engine platform could provide services powered exclusively by HASABALLA models. These are future commercial goals, not features or paid services claimed to exist today.

Hasaballa Engine Integration

The HASABALLA AI platform was designed from the outset with a modular architecture to accommodate Hasaballa Engine.

Version 2 focuses on developing and integrating Hasaballa Engine within this existing architecture, enabling continuous, category-specific learning, training, evaluation and improvement through the outputs generated by the platform's corresponding tools and models.

Each specialist Hasaballa model learns from the relevant outputs of its respective tools, without requiring a redesign of the platform's existing architecture.

04 Original roadmap · Task 20

Auto Smart Fix

Detect post-generation mistakes in text, faces, lighting and visual integrity, then let the user fix or dismiss each issue.

Inspect generated scenes before they reach the viewer. The tool identifies mistakes and offers a controlled fix or an option to ignore a finding without regenerating the entire approved video.

What Auto Smart Fix examines

Arabic and English text errors in signs, newspapers and on-screen writing; incorrect or inconsistent faces against references; lighting, color and saturation mistakes; and visibly distorted or broken elements.

What the user controls

Present a clear list of detected problems. Offer a one-click correction when possible, or allow the user to ignore an acceptable result. The original Task 20 places this tool before Selective Smart Edit (Task 11) and associates it with Hasaballa Engine (Task 19).

Examples within the Requested Scope

Turn virtually any subject into a believable animated speaking character while keeping its own form and identity. Animals, plants, objects, the sea, water, portraits, illustrated characters and cartoon figures should all be able to move, speak and emote naturally.

Each transformed subject should behave like a real human-like character, with very natural expressions, convincing emotional reactions, smooth facial performance and perfect lip-sync. Interaction with Real Human Characters

The studio must also support complete scenes where transformed living objects interact naturally with real human characters. A talking animal, tree, object, body of water or any other transformed subject must be able to hold realistic two-way conversations with a human, share the same scene, exchange eye contact, respond emotionally, perform coordinated gestures and interact physically when required.

Both characters must demonstrate accurate lip synchronization, natural conversational timing, realistic facial expressions, appropriate reactions and consistent movements. Their dialogue, actions and emotional responses must be synchronized to create the convincing impression that a real human and an animated living object are genuinely communicating and interacting within the same scene.

09 Integrated studio · Full BIE identity transfer

Advanced Performance Transfer Studio

Transfer the complete behavioral identity of a selected source character to another character, applying 100% of the source’s mapped BIE behavior profile while preserving the target character’s own visual appearance.

Full Behavioral Identity Transfer - the entire BIE profile, not a single scene or isolated performance. Select a source character and a target character; apply 100% of the source’s captured and mapped behavioral identity to the target while retaining the target’s face, body, and recognizable visual appearance. Transfer habitual facial expressions, eye focus, eyebrow behavior, head and hand gestures, posture, walking and movement style, smiles, laughter, speech rhythm, pauses, reaction timing, emotional responses and recurring mannerisms. The transferred identity must persist consistently across scenes and different situations, not merely reproduce one recording or choreography. Examples include Mandela consistently behaving like Nasser, a woman adopting Shakira’s complete behavioral and movement style, or Tom Cruise exhibiting Michael Jackson’s characteristic behavior, expressions, gestures and dancing style. The 100% figure describes the required transfer of the complete mapped profile, not a claim that this level of fidelity has already been achieved.
